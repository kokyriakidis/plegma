# Consensus polishing (2026-10-22)

## Where the VNTR errors came from

The sets: 180 read sets from real vntr-eval haplotypes (hap32, not CHM13 or GRCh38), 30 regions over five strata,
10 or 30 reads each at 1, 5 or 10% uniform error (`vntr_reads.py`). Errors are the consensus's edits against the
truth (karan, unit costs), sorted by `errors.py`: substitutions, indels in or beside a run of three or more of one
letter (homopolymer), other indels inside a tandem repeat (a 12-mer through the position recurring within 500
letters), and the rest.

`oracle.py` places every read against the truth and lets each truth column vote. It makes 4 errors over the 180
sets, 1 of them not in a homopolymer: the reads hold the truth nearly everywhere, and the errors below are the MSA's
and its consensus's. Almost all of them fall in the sets of 10 reads at 10% error, where a missing letter or unit in
a repeat is an insertion in one slot for some reads, another slot or a substitution for others, each a minority in
its column. Their edit distances to the consensus agree all the same.

## What mends them

`plegma/polish.mojo`, `-P` (on by default in global mode, at most 50 rounds): every round aligns each read to the
consensus and keeps the one-letter and whole-unit edits that lower the reads' summed edit distance, scored exactly
from each read's forward and backward edit matrices in a band around its alignment, the consensus cut into segments
outside tandem repeats.

Times are the whole run of every set, 8 at once on the i9-7900X, other jobs sharing the machine.

Earlier attempts, in Python on the same sets: a column vote of the reads aligned to the consensus (as against the
truth in the oracle) left 64 non-homopolymer errors, with indels left-normalized too; one-letter edits scored in a
fixed window of 30 letters each side left 16. A window misses a read whose indel the alignment placed in another
copy of the repeat, outside it; the banded forward and backward matrices see it wherever it is. A missing unit of a
repeat is not mended a letter at a time: one letter of it alone raised the reads' summed distance (347 to 353 in
the test case), the whole unit lowered it to 292, the truth's.

## Simulated sets

The 74 simulated sets up to 100 kb (`simulate.py`, uniform and ONT-like errors): plegma -p -R 1 -P 0 1,415 errors,
with polishing 284 (35 to 53 sets exact); abPOA 1,277 on the 63 it finishes. The rest are mostly the ONT-like sets'
homopolymer runs (worst, `sim_ont_L30000_N10_r10`: 108).

## The threshold

An edit is tried where, within two letters, two reads disagree with the consensus and at least `share` of them.
At 0, 0.5 and 0.7 the simulated sets keep the same 282 errors (one-letter edits), the VNTR sets 31, 31 and 30; at
1.0, 342 and 52. 0.5 is the default; it cut the VNTR run from 41.7 to 32.0 s against 0.

## What is left, and why

`profile_errors.py` compares, for every set still wrong, the reads' summed edit distance to the consensus with
theirs to the truth. In every one, on all three benchmarks, the consensus's is lower or equal: the polishing finds
the optimum of its objective, and the objective is what is wrong (`search` 0 sets, `objective` all).

| benchmark | plegma -p -R 1 | homopolymer | other kinds | oracle (every set, 1 Mb too) | abPOA (the sets it finishes) |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| VNTR read sets (180) | 24 | 19 | 5 | 4 | 357 |
| uniform simulation (up to 100 kb) | 87 | 54 | 33 | 15 | 678 |
| ONT-like simulation (up to 100 kb) | 197 | 191 | 6 | 77 | 599 |

Unit edit distance counts a read's best alignment alone; a likelihood counts them all, and a read one letter short
in a run of L can be aligned L ways. `likelihood.py` scores each wrong set under a pair HMM (k inserted letters
pi^k (1 - pi), each uniform; a template letter deleted pd, else read as itself 1 - ps), its rates estimated from
the reads' alignments to the consensus, every alignment summed in a band. It prefers the truth in 17 of 18 VNTR
sets and 6 of 6 uniform sets: polishing on that likelihood should mend most of what is left there.

The ONT-like sets (`simulate.py --model ont`) change whole homopolymer runs by one or two letters, a run of h
letters with probability proportional to h (1 + 0.5 (h - 1)), up to 0.9: long runs are mostly misread, and with 10
reads even the oracle misses 77. The plain HMM prefers the truth in 3 of 9 wrong sets, one with deletions by run
membership and insertions copying a neighbour 2 of 9: what they need is a model of run lengths, each run's length
called from the reads' run lengths through P(observed | true) estimated from the data.
