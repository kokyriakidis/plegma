## How vntr-eval scores an MSA (implied.py, reproduces its numbers exactly)

A pair of paths through the MSA's graph is aligned optimally between the nodes they share, the identical letters
the MSA stacks: mismatches and gap placement cost nothing of their own, only wrongly stacked letters do; and every
pair of the region's 34 paths counts, so a sequence 22 paths spell weighs 22 times. plegma's refinement counted each
distinct sequence once: on L011121 it fixed rare pairs and broke the pair a singleton makes with that sequence.
Weighting the objective by multiplicity (refine, progressive, the copy start) fixed it: unit_aware's wins on both
metrics went from 3 regions to 1.

## Search or objective (diagnose.py)

After weighting, every region unit_aware still won on unit cost was lower on plegma's own objective: not a search
failure but the objective trading unit cost for fewer gap openings. Pricing mismatches, gap letters and openings
apart (`-W`) traces the trade-off: 1,1,1 keeps affine low but lost unit cost at hotspot_other; 1,1,0 wins unit
cost everywhere (1.023 at hotspot VNTRs) at 1.44 affine; 2,2,1 (an opening at half a mismatch) beats unit_aware on
both in every stratum.

## Anchors (experiments/anchors)

Every seed anchor of a pair checked against all its optimal unit-cost alignments, and each plan charged the excess
its anchors force (the best alignment through them minus the optimum), 24 regions, 30 pairs each:

| source | letters off an optimal alignment | forced excess |
| --- | --: | --: |
| MUMs outside repeats (the flanks) | 2.2% | +0.014% |
| MUMs inside, one-indel rule (`backbone(one_indel=2)`) | 4.0% | +0.24% |
| MUMs unique in a repeat window (placement's first source) | 12.4% | +1.83% |
| SMEMs of 31 (its second) | 10.4% | +3.79% |
| optimal two-piece alignment (its third, and refine's band centres) | 16.7% | +12.19% |
| copies paired whole, runs of 12 or more | 19.1% | +5.99% |
| optimal unit-cost alignment | 0 | 0 |

Inside a repeat a MUM splitting its window's copy-number difference is wrong three times in four; one at either end
far less often; what remains wrong sits in windows differing by over 1,000 letters, where the side of the indel is
the open question. The correct anchors are the unit-cost alignment's, which karan computes faster than the seed
recursion at these sizes: placement from those votes alone wins 6 regions of the quick 24 and loses 2 (L009658 1.247
to 1.211, placement only), and through the refinement matches the seeded cascade on the 149, a little faster.

## Tried and dropped

- Holding anchors' letters to their columns (`-V ,,h`): worse in every stratum, with seeds or with the alignments'
  consensus (L014210 placement 1.095 to 1.164): it locks in the placed rows' errors.
- Nearest-first order (`-V ,,,1`): a better placement (hotspot VNTR 1.096 to 1.088), none after refinement.
- The even-weight third start: better on both in 7 regions, none worse, at twice the time (kept, on by default only
  where a sequence repeats).
- Copy-guided anchors: pairing copies whole costs 2 to 3 times the optimum on short units (L013064, period 8).

The refinement erases most differences between starts; the objective it lowers decides the result.
